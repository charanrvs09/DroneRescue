DRONERESCUE FIRE EMERGENCY PROTOCOL

Purpose:
Provide operational guidance for drone-assisted fire verification and emergency response.

1. INITIAL RESPONSE
- Treat a fire alert as an incident requiring verification.
- The Incident Agent should evaluate alert confidence before dispatch.
- Dispatch should only occur when the incident meets the configured dispatch threshold.

2. DRONE SELECTION
- Prefer an available drone with sufficient battery.
- For fire verification, prefer drones equipped with both RGB and thermal cameras.
- Do not select an offline or unavailable drone.

3. INITIAL OBSERVATION
- Take off only after safety checks pass.
- Navigate to the incident coordinates.
- Capture an RGB image for visual verification.
- Analyze the image for flames, smoke, structural damage, and other emergency indicators.

4. THERMAL VERIFICATION
- If RGB analysis indicates a high-confidence fire, perform thermal inspection.
- Thermal imagery should be used to identify hot regions and support fire severity assessment.
- A thermal inspection must not be executed if the selected drone lacks a thermal camera.

5. LOW-CONFIDENCE OBSERVATIONS
- If vision confidence is below the configured threshold, do not escalate immediately.
- Reposition the drone when appropriate.
- Capture another observation.
- Re-evaluate the new evidence before making an emergency decision.

6. SAFETY REQUIREMENTS
- Maintain minimum battery reserves.
- Never bypass the Safety Governor.
- Unsafe actions must be blocked.
- High-risk emergency actions require human approval.

7. MISSION COMPLETION
- After required observations are completed, return the drone to its designated home location.
- Record mission actions, observations, safety decisions, and outcomes for later review.

8. IMPORTANT RULE
AI agents provide recommendations and decisions, but deterministic safety controls remain authoritative over drone execution.
